#### Interlaced films are converted without deinterlacing, and nothing says so

*Severity: medium.*

**Evidence.** A 720x576 MPEG-2 film with field order "tb" (made by scripts/make-films.sh): Play answers method convert, setting 1, lost [] and the stream arrives as 720x576 flagged progressive (both converters; plays and jumps in headless Chromium). Running the converter's own picture chain on it (fps, scale, setsar, format=yuv420p, as internal/hls/convert.go builds it) and then ffmpeg's idet filter on the result: 200 of 200 frames detected as interlaced, i.e. the comb lines are baked into the converted picture. internal/server/convert.go never reads PictureFacts.FieldOrder (grep: no use outside internal/hls/facts.go) and convertArgs has no bwdif although Abilities.Bwdif is read. The commit's "Not yet" list names HDR, speeds and subtitles only. DVD and TV material in MPEG-2 is the commonest kind of film this commit starts to play.

**Suggested.** Until slice 3 is built, list it under "not yet" in docs/decisions.md. The design's line is bwdif=mode=send_frame:parity=auto:deint=interlaced in front of fps when the facts say tt/bb/tb/bt and Abilities.Bwdif is true (work for whoever holds internal/hls; convertSpec must pass the fact on). Add an interlaced film to the server test and check with idet.

**Suggested.** One rule in both places: for a picture that is taller than wide once turned, fit it lying down and swap back (as playback.fit does), either in convertSpec or in hls Size (the hls helper). Add upright rows to TestTheSettingsOfAConversion and a rotated film to the Play test.

#### Every list and every Play waits behind one lock while ffmpeg is asked what it can do

*Severity: medium.*

**Evidence.** abilities() holds conversion.mu while ffmpeg runs (30 s limit); judge() and handlePlay call it. With a stand-in ffmpeg that takes 6 s per question the first GET /api/movies after start took 12.9 s (both converters' runs); with one that hangs, the first list was still unanswered after 12 s. After a failed ask, the first request a minute later asks again inside the request while all others queue. The released code's lists never touch ffmpeg. The comment on askConverterSoon says neither the first list nor Play waits. Real asks are quick when warm (shipped 8.1.3: 11 ms for both questions, 6.1.1: 123 ms); a cold start of the 110 MB program on Windows could not be measured here. Closing during the ask is fine (0 to 1 ms, the waiting list is answered at once). Retries are once a minute (1 ask over 12 lists; 1 more for 8 lists at once after the minute), but no test holds that: changing the retry to never or to at-once is not noticed. Reproduce: copy: LM_BREAK_MEDIA=<films> go test ./internal/server -run TestZZAskingTheConverter -v.

**Suggested.** Never run ffmpeg under the lock inside a request: ask in the background at start and on a timer after a failure; let a request wait a second or two at most for the first answer and otherwise answer from what is known (a list could say "unknown" rather than "no"). Add a test with a slow stand-in.

## 3. The picture converter: two independent checks and a diagnosis

State: the cause of the test that fails with the shipped converter is found (below). ### The diagnosis of the failure on the shipped converter

#### Sound passed through beside a converted picture is written before the picture it belongs with once it is 10 s ahead (ffmpeg's -max_interleave_delta); the shipped 8.1.3 crosses that limit on the 'frozen' film, 6.1.1 stayed 0.24 s under it by luck

*Severity: high.*

**Evidence.** REPRODUCED on 8.1.3 (pinned): go test ./internal/hls -run TestEveryConvertedChunkBeginsWhereTheListSays at 3b5dbf3 fails only in /frozen, sound passed through: 'chunk 1 played through: picture and sound start -0.200 s apart', 'jumped to: -0.243', 'chunk 0 differs: played through 90/151 frames, jumped to 90/153'. It is a race: the exact command run by hand misplaced sound in 4 of 8 and 8 of 12 runs on 8.1.3, 0 of 16 on 6.1.1. Whole-set baseline at 3b5dbf3: 8.1.3 fails this one test, 6.1.1 passes all; go test ./... on 8.1.3 fails nothing else.

WHAT THE PACKETS SHOW (fragment table from the moof boxes, times carry the +1 s shift): good run: frag0 picture 1.000..4.000, sound 0.979..4.008 (142 packets); bad run (8.1.3): frag0 sound 0.979..4.243 (153 packets), frag1 picture 4.000.., sound 4.243.. (130). So 11 AAC packets (0.235 s) that belong after the cut at 3 s were written before the key frame at 3 s; the mp4 writer closes a fragment when the key frame arrives, so they land in chunk 0.

CAUSE, EXACTLY: (1) libavformat writes packets in time order across tracks and waits for a track that has nothing only until the queued packets span more than max_interleave_delta (10 s); then it writes without it. With -loglevel debug 8.1.3 prints 'Delay between the first packet and last packet in the muxing queue is 10005333 > 10000000: forcing output' 5 to 26 times in EVERY run of this film; 6.1.1 prints it 0 times. (2) A converted picture reaches the writer later than its sound: 5 frames held by the decoder's threads, 1 by the fps filter, 17 by x264 (trace: encoder had taken 116 frames when the writer had 99 packets) = about 0.75 s; and through a stretch where the file holds no frames the fps filter cannot make the fill frames until the next real frame is read. The frozen film has no frame from 4 to 13 s, so the picture at the writer stands at 3.27..3.40 s while sound arrives up to 13.16 s. (3) On 6.1.1 reading, decoding, encoding and writing go in lockstep; -debug_ts trace: the most the sound was ever ahead was 9.76 s (writer: picture to 3.400, sound to 13.163), 0.24 s under the limit. Confirmed by one-variable change: 6.1.1 with -max_interleave_delta 9.8 s and 9.95 s still forced nothing. (4) On 8.1.3 the reader is its own thread and copied sound goes from it straight to the writer; it reads the 9 silent-picture seconds in no time while the encoder is still working on frames before 3 s. When the sound front passes 13.0 s and the key frame for 3 s has not come out of the encoder yet, sound from 3.0 s on is forced out ahead of it; how much (0.07..0.243 s) is how far the reader got before the key frame arrived. (5) Converted sound passes on 8.1.3 only because it reaches the writer about 0.36 s later than copied sound (trace: reader at 13.013 s, writer's sound at 12.651 s), which kept it under 10 s: also luck. (6) Only this film, because only it has a stretch without frames; in the others the sound is never more than about 0.75 s ahead. 'longsound' already had the flag.

*Severity: high.*

**Evidence.** When reading the film stops early, ffmpeg complains but ends with status 0 and writes well-formed files holding only the captions it got to; Extract then marks every track Done and Load accepts the folder. (1) Read error: 175 s film with 1,700 styled lines, reads made to fail with an input/output error after half the file's bytes (LD_PRELOAD fault, source at internal/subs/testdata/checker/eio.c on my check branch). ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.3 both print 'Read error' / 'Error during demuxing: Input/output error' and exit 0. Extract returned err=nil, 4 tracks Done=true, s0.ass had 875 of 1,700 lines, progress went 0..50 then 100, Load=nil. (2) File cut in half ('File ended prematurely', exit 0): same outcome. Repro without the fault tool: LM_FINDINGS=1 go test -run TestFindingHalfAFilmReadCountsAsFinished ./internal/subs/ fails on both converters: 'the reading stopped 49% of the way through a film of 60023 ms, 29 of 50 captions came out, and the result is marked finished'. Measured too: with -xerror the read error exits 251 on both, the cut file still exits 0.

**Suggested.** Do not rely on the exit status alone. Extract already knows the film's length, how far its clock got, and what ffmpeg wrote to stderr at -loglevel error: refuse the run (or mark the Result partial so the caller tries again later) when stderr reports a read/demux error or the clock ended well short of the length. Not -xerror alone: it would turn one undecodable text line into a failed pass. The clock test alone can misfire when the first sound track is shorter than the film.

#### One track ffmpeg cannot handle makes the whole film's extraction fail, every time (both converters)

*Severity: high.*

**Evidence.** (a) MKV with a styled track, a SubRip track in an 8-bit Greek encoding (every line invalid UTF-8; made with '-f srt -i greek.srt -c:s copy') and a PGS track: ffmpeg exits 69 ('Decode error rate 1 exceeds maximum 0.666667'), Extract returns an error and writes no index, so the good styled and PGS tracks are lost too. (b) Same film with valid text but one PGS piece's length field set to 0xFFFF: exit 183 ('[sup] Not enough data ... Error muxing a packet'). Repros fail on both converters: TestFindingOneBadTextTrackLosesTheWholeFilm, TestFindingOneDamagedPictureTrackLosesTheWholeFilm (LM_FINDINGS=1). M3 reads each track by itself (internal/server/helpers.go:211), so wiring this in unchanged would be a step back for such films. The author listed 'no fallback when one track breaks the pass' as not done and said no such film could be made; these two recipes make one. Measured: '-max_error_rate 1' lets (a) end with status 0 on both converters, but the bad track is then a 7-byte WebVTT with no captions that would be marked Done.

**Suggested.** When the pass fails, retry track by track (or without the output ffmpeg's message names) and mark only the bad track not Done. For text tracks consider copying them as they are and converting in Go with the package's decodeText, which also repairs the next problem.

#### Lines of a text track in an old encoding are dropped without a word and the track is marked done (both converters)

*Severity: medium.*

**Evidence.** SubRip track with 2 of 5 lines in Latin-1 inside an MKV: t1.vtt holds 3 captions, Done=true, ffmpeg exit 0. TestFindingLinesInAnOldEncodingAreDroppedSilently fails on both converters (an earlier probe lost 4 of 10). M3 does the same today for tracks inside a film, so this is not a step back, but it is a result that looks complete and is not, and docs/decisions.md lists only the beside-the-film case as known.

**Suggested.** Copy text tracks out unconverted and convert in Go (decodeText already handles Windows-1252), or at least count ffmpeg's 'Invalid UTF-8' complaints for the track and record that lines were lost.

#### Flattener: letter-by-letter signs and template karaoke come out as piles of fragments, and roles cannot separate them from the translation

*Severity: medium.*

**Evidence.** Sample of typical typesetting (TestProbeFlattenTypesetting, 251 lines in, 28 captions out). As a person would expect: a sign tracked over 48 frames in 3 layers with a mask drawing gives 1 caption; a gradient of 20 clipped strips gives 1; two speakers at once give 2 in order; plain \k karaoke gives whole lines. Not as expected: (a) a sign set one letter per line ('OPEN') gives four one-letter captions at the same moment, which a player stacks upward; (b) karaoke made by a template (source line kept as a Comment with effect 'karaoke', each syllable its own line with effect 'fx') gives 'so','ra','no','i','ro' as five captions all showing 24.7 to 29.0 s, and both they and the English translation line get role Song, so a caller cannot keep the translation and drop the syllables by role; (c) 'I...{\alpha&HFF&} love you.' followed by the full line gives one caption that shows the hidden words a second early. (a) and (b) are in the author's list of limits; the role consequence of (b), and (c), are not.

**Suggested.** (b) take the Comment line whose effect is 'karaoke' as the caption and drop lines whose effect is 'fx', or give those a role of their own; (a) join pinned one-glyph lines with identical times in script order, or mark them; (c) leave out text that follows \alpha&HFF& until alpha is set back.

#### Flattener prints pen movements for drawings begun in ways renderers accept

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** TestFindingDrawingsThatComeOutAsCaptions: '{\p1.5}m 0 0 l 10 10 20 20{\p0}', '{\p 1}m 0 0 l 10 10', '{\p1 mask}m 0 0 l 10 10', '{\p1)}m 0 0 l 10 10' each come out as the caption 'm 0 0 l 10 10 ...' (also '{\t(\p1)}'). Renderers read the number after \p as far as it is one and ignore the rest. Related: '{\p1}m 0 0{\p-1}after' loses 'after'; an unclosed brace is printed with the instruction and a literal \N ('text {\i1 never closed\Nsecond line' comes out unchanged). All rare in real scripts.

**Suggested.** Read the number after \p as renderers do: optional spaces, leading integer, ignore what follows, negative counts as 0. Process \N in text after an unclosed brace.

#### Several tests pass with the thing they claim to test removed

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** 73 single changes run on both converters; 58 were noticed on both. Not noticed on either: (1) Load's rule that a file name in the index must be one this package gives, for tracks and for fonts: all 11 cases of TestLoadRefusesAFolderThatIsNotWhole are refused because the file has another length; with the name comparison deleted that test still passes, while my TestFindingLoadRefusesAForeignNameOfTheRightLength fails (it passes on the real code). (2) The film's path made absolute: TestPathsThatAreNotWholeStillLead uses the stand-in, which never opens the film. (3) The WebVTT heading check and the WebVTT final-line-end check: the one 'cut-vtt' case is caught by either, so neither is tested alone. (4) First caption of a text track being the earliest, not the first line found. (5) Errors inside a Matroska cluster when the file must be whole. (6) Both Sync calls and the context check after the pass (not testable without fault injection). (7) Flattener: 3-digit fractions of a second, and script order after joining. Noticed on 6.1.1 but not on the shipped 8.1.3: '100 only after the index is written' (TestOnePassWritesEveryTrack notices only because 6.1.1's clock passes the film's length; the stand-in clock stops at 99.9 s), and fix (a), which by its nature shows only on 6.1.1.

**Suggested.** Add the Load test from checker_findings_test.go; give the stand-in a clock line beyond the film's length; add a real-ffmpeg run with a relative film path; split the cut-vtt case in two (no heading; no final line end).

#### A DVD track from a .vob/.mpg file is recorded with size 0 by 0

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** Film made with '-c:s dvdsub -f vob': ffprobe gives N/A for the track's size; Extract writes p0.mks with Width:0 Height:0, Done=true, on both converters (such a track has no description text in the file). The same captions in MP4 or MKV give 1920x1080. The author's report says only an empty PGS track gives size 0.

**Suggested.** Say so in PictureFacts' and Track's comments, and have the burn step fall back to the film's picture size when the size is 0.

#### Extract into a folder that already holds a result leaves the older files, fonts included

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** Extracted a film with 4 tracks and 2 fonts into a folder, then another film (1 styled track, 1 font) into the same folder: the folder then holds the new index, s0.ass and fonts/f0 plus the first film's fonts/f1, p2.sup, p3.mks and t1.vtt. Load is correct (it lists only the new files), but 'fontsdir=fonts' would load the other film's font. The design gives each film and stamp its own folder, so this needs a re-run into a used folder (for instance after the index version is raised).

**Suggested.** Before moving files in, remove from the folder every file with one of the package's own names that the new result does not list; or state in Extract's comment that the folder must be fresh.

#### Fonts: a claimed font is kept whatever its bytes, there is no size limit, and fonts past the 300th vanish without a mark

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** checkFont removes an attachment only if it neither begins like a font nor claims to be one ('!looksLikeFont && !claimsFont'), so anything named *.ttf or typed font/* is kept; the author's report says the first four bytes decide. A 40 MB attachment typed font/ttf was written whole (probe with 200 fonts; fine functionally). plan() silently stops at maxFonts=300 and nothing in Result says fonts were left out (tracks past the 100th are at least listed as not done).

**Suggested.** Decide a per-font and per-film size limit, or record the choice not to have one; record in Result when fonts were left out.

#### On ffmpeg 6.1.1 only: a caption line left at the end of a script's heading without a line end makes the film fail for good

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** The author's 'tail' film with its trailing 'Data: ...' line patched in place to a 'Dialogue: ...' line (same length, no line end): on 6.1.1 Extract returns 'subtitle track 0 was not written whole: the file is cut short'; on 8.1.3 it succeeds because that converter adds the line end. So fix (a) still refuses a file that 6.1.1 wrote whole. Not reachable with the converter that ships.

**Suggested.** Accept it as a limit of the PATH converter, or tell the two cases apart by whether the last line is one ffmpeg wrote or part of the heading's tail.

#### Small things in the readers and in what is kept from a film

*Severity: low.*

**Evidence.** (1) ReadPicture took 11.7 s on a 40 MB .mks made only of empty elements (one read call per element, no limit, no context) and it accepts '.mkv' as well; it matters only if a later step hands it files from the library. (4) substest writes a run of exactly 2 pixels as a 3-byte run, which the format's description reserves for 3 or more; both converters decode it exactly.

**Suggested.** Cap the number of elements ReadPicture walks; strip control characters in clip(); have the caller strip the data folder from errors.
