# Your checklist for M3 (about 20 minutes)

M3 is the version meant for daily use. Until now a file your browser could not play simply did not play, or played without sound. Now the PC repackages such files while you watch, and converts their sound if it has to. The picture is never changed, so nothing is lost in quality and the PC hardly notices.

What still does not play: files whose **picture** is in a format your device cannot show. Those are marked in the list, and are what M4 is for.

## 1. Install

1. Open https://github.com/Matiaspnyhus/Local-media-project/releases/latest on the PC, download **LocalMedia-Setup.exe** from the list of files at the bottom, and run it over the top of the old one. (The download is bigger than last time: it now includes the converter.) Windows shows a blue warning because the installer is not signed yet: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
2. Your library, sign-in and watch history are kept.
3. This is the last time you install by hand. From the next version on, the app updates itself (you can see and switch this under **Settings**).

## 2. On the PC, in your usual browser

1. Find a film that used to be silent or marked "Needs converting" because of its sound (usually an .mkv with Dolby or DTS sound) and play it.
   - It should start within a few seconds, **with sound**.
2. Drag the timeline to somewhere in the middle. It should carry on within about 3 seconds.
3. Under the player, open **Why is this converting?** It should say in plain words what the PC is doing and why.
4. If the film has more than one sound track, there is a **Sound** list under the player. Pick another. The film carries on from the same place in the other language.
5. If the film has subtitles, there is a **Subtitles** list. Switch one on. Then jump far ahead: the subtitles should still match what is said.
   - The first time you use subtitles that are stored inside a big film, they can take a little while to appear after a jump far ahead. The second time they are instant.
6. Press **Back**, then play the same film again. It should resume where you stopped, with the same sound and subtitles.
7. Only if you have any: play a **TV recording** (a file ending in `.ts` or `.m2ts`). The first time, the PC reads the whole recording once to learn where it can be cut, which can take up to a minute for a long one on a hard disk. If it takes longer than 20 seconds the film starts anyway. From the second time on it starts at once.

## 3. On your iPhone

An iPhone cannot open .mkv files at all, so until now most of your library would not play there.

1. Open Local Media on the iPhone (same address as before) and play an .mkv film.
2. It should play, with sound. Try full screen, and try dragging the timeline.
3. Switch subtitles on from the list under the player.
4. Lock the phone for a minute, unlock it and press play. It should carry on.

## 4. A show

1. Play an episode of a show, pick a sound track or subtitles, and watch a minute.
2. Play the next episode. It should start with the same choice.

## 5. Your preferences

1. On the library page, click **Settings**. The first box is **Sound and subtitles**.
2. Choose the language you want sound in, and when you want subtitles. These are yours: another person signing in has their own.
3. Open **What this device can play** in the same box. You do not need to understand the list; it is there so I can see what your devices can do when you send me a support bundle.

## 6. If the PC is a laptop (from version 0.3.1)

A laptop goes to sleep by itself, and a sleeping PC sends no films. From version 0.3.1 the app asks Windows to stay awake while someone is watching. Two things are still yours:

1. **Keep the laptop plugged in** while it is sending films. On battery, Windows may put it to sleep after a few minutes whatever the app asks.
2. **Closing the lid** puts a laptop to sleep, and no app can change that. If you want films to keep playing with the lid closed:
   - Open **Settings**, then **System**, then **Power & battery**, then **Lid, power & sleep button controls**. At **Closing the lid will make my PC**, in the box under **Plugged in**, choose **Do Nothing**. Leave the box under **On battery** as it is. (The names can differ a little between versions of Windows.)
   - If you cannot find that: open the Start menu, type **Control Panel**, open it, choose **Hardware and Sound**, then **Power Options**, then **Choose what closing the lid does** on the left. In the row **When I close the lid**, under **Plugged in**, choose **Do nothing**, then **Save changes**.
   - With this setting the laptop keeps running with the lid shut, and gets warm. Do not put it in a bag while it is on.
3. To check: plug the laptop in, leave its lid open, start a film on the iPhone, and do not touch the laptop for 30 minutes. The film should play through. If it stops, tell me roughly after how many minutes; the support bundle shows me when the PC fell asleep.
